&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Confidentiality of communications in peer-to-peer networks is a fundamental requirement for systems where participants must be protected from surveillance. Existing anonymization methods either create significant delays, are vulnerable to traffic analysis, or require specialized infrastructure.

## The Challenge

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In systems where participant anonymity is a condition of legitimacy — for example, in secret voting — encrypting message content is insufficient. It is also necessary to conceal the transmission route, metadata, and patterns of participant activity. Traffic analysis can de-anonymize participants even when message content is encrypted.

## The Solution

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A message anonymization technology has been developed for peer-to-peer networks that eliminates the possibility of establishing a link between a participant and their actions through traffic or metadata analysis. The technology is integrated into the base network architecture and requires no additional infrastructure or specialized nodes.
